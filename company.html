Company

Who does the work, and how

A buyer should know who they are dealing with before they write. This page is the plain account, including the parts a larger firm would leave out.

The company

BCCS is a programme of KRYONIS Sovereign Systems Limited, incorporated in Hong Kong SAR. Contracts and invoices are in that company’s name.

The founder is Steven Alber, who directs strategy and the programme. The company is small. There is no sales team: the person who answers your message is the person responsible for the work.

How the work is done

Much of the engineering is done by software agents working under written instructions, in stages that each leave their own records: a method file, the runs of an automated test system, an acceptance note. The public record says so itself. The internal acceptance of Demonstrator 001 is attributed to “The build agent of the measurement stage and the programme's own CI”.

That is the reason a record is built the way it is. It does not ask you to trust who or what did the computing. The inputs, the rule and the steps are stored so that someone else can do it again.

What BCCS is not

  • Not an accredited validation and verification body, and not seeking to act as one.
  • Not a laboratory, a university group or a standards body.
  • Not independently validated: no outside scientist has yet reviewed the method. See below.
  • Not a company with customers to point to. There are none yet, and the programme record says so.

Independence and money

  • The fee for a record never depends on its outcome.
  • Who commissioned a record and who paid for it is written in the record.
  • Payment is by invoice. No part of an evidence engagement involves a token, a licence or a blockchain transaction.
  • KRYONIS also publishes a protocol concept that includes a token and node licences. It is described, with the state of every part, on the protocol research page. The evidence service does not depend on it.

The name

Earlier documents expand the letters BCCS in more than one way. On this site BCCS is used as a name, and the service is described by what it does.

What a reviewer can be given

The withheld files: the class maps of each evidence group, the agreement table and the canonical result record. They are held back from the public because the method refused to state an extent. A reviewer receives them under a short written agreement.

What happens to a review

It is recorded with the claim, whatever it concludes, under your name if you wish. External review is budgeted in the programme plan, which is not funded; until it is, a review is unpaid. Nobody is paid for a favourable one.
