Tool

Could someone else check your area claim?

Twelve points, in four groups. Answer for one claim: the hectares in a report, a baseline, a restoration milestone. Your answers stay in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you decide to add the summary to a request.

The claim

1. The claim is one sentence with a quantity, a place and a period

A claim that cannot be stated in one sentence cannot be tested in one piece.

2. The boundary exists as a file, with a version and a date

Most disputes about area begin with two parties measuring different shapes.

3. It is written down what the claim does not cover

Readers assume the widest meaning unless they are told the narrow one.

The data

4. Every dataset behind the figure is listed with its source, date and version

“Satellite data” is not a source. A scene identifier is.

5. The files are stored, and you can show they have not changed

A checksum recorded at the time is enough. A fresh download later is a different input.

6. A third party could obtain the same inputs without asking you

If the inputs live only with the author, nobody else can repeat the work.

The method

7. The method was written down before the result was produced

Otherwise nobody, including you, can show that it was not adjusted to fit.

8. Thresholds and other choices are stated, with the reason for each

Every threshold is a choice. An unstated choice looks like a hidden one.

9. The figure has been regenerated from the stored inputs by someone who did not produce it

This is the difference between “we computed” and “it can be computed”.

10. Uncertainty is given as a range, and the main choices have been varied to see if the answer holds

A single number with no range claims a precision nobody has.

Review

11. Someone outside the project has examined the method and the result

Internal checks catch slips. They do not supply independence.

12. It is recorded who commissioned and who paid for the work behind the figure

Readers will ask. It is better that the record answers first.

Your summary

What this is. A structured list of your own answers. It carries no score and no judgement of your project, and it says nothing about whether the claim is true.

How to read the result

Points one to six are housekeeping. They cost time, not expertise, and a project can usually put them right itself.

Points seven to ten are where claims fail under examination. A method written after the result, an unvaried threshold, a figure only its author has produced: these are what a verifier, a rating analyst or a journalist will find.

Points eleven and twelve cannot be fixed from the inside. They need someone else.

The checklist follows the way the BCCS method treats its own records, including the parts where the demonstrator still answers “no”.
