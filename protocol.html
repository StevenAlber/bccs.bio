Protocol research · concept

The BCCS protocol is a design, not an operating network

In April 2026 KRYONIS published a protocol concept: an identifier for biological assets, a network of verification nodes and a token on the Base chain. The documents remain here, unchanged. This page says what state each part is in.

The short version

Two contracts are deployed on Base. No verification network operates. No biological asset has been verified through the protocol and no attestation exists. The pages of April 2026 describe the design in the present tense; where they differ from this page, this page and the status page of KRYONIS (opens kryonis.global) take precedence.

How the concept relates to the evidence service

The protocol asks a large question: how could the verified state of a living asset be recorded and relied on between institutions?

The evidence service answers a smaller and earlier one: for one claim about one area, what does the evidence actually support? The BCCS Evidence Observatory (opens observatory.bccs.bio) is where that question has been worked through on real data.

The answer so far is sobering and useful. In the protocol’s own lifecycle the demonstrator site stands at state 01, Unverified: the public record claims no “lifecycle state other than 01 Unverified”, no “completed production PoPS verification” and no “lifecycle transition or a Base attestation”.

A verification layer is only as good as the evidence underneath it. That is why the evidence work comes first, and why it is offered on its own.

What an evidence engagement does not involve

  • No token and no licence
  • No wallet and no blockchain transaction
  • No payment other than by invoice
  • No reliance on any part of the table above
