Who it is for

Three situations where someone must act on an area claim

These are the uses BCCS was built for. They are hypotheses, stated as such in the programme record: there is no customer yet, and none is named here.

Funders, development programmes, evaluators

You release money against a restoration or protection milestone

The implementer reports the hectares. You have the report, perhaps a map, and a deadline. An evaluation years later may find that the area standing is a fraction of the area reported, and by then the money has moved.

What a record adds

  • An area check that does not come from the implementer and that your own evaluator can re-run.
  • A date and a validity window: the record says which period it covers and nothing about any other.
  • An explicit outcome. If the satellite evidence cannot settle the question, you are told that instead of being given a number.

Where it stops

A planting that is one or two years old does not show as canopy from orbit. For early survival you need field counts. A record becomes informative for established stands, for baselines before a project starts, and for loss.

Site evidence check · Ask about a site

A question worth asking your implementer

“If we handed your area figure, your boundary file and your data to a stranger, could they arrive at the same number without calling you?”

If the answer is no, the figure rests on trust. That may be acceptable. It should be a decision and not an accident.

Project developers, verification bodies, rating agencies, insurers

You want to know whether a claim will survive examination

A verification visit, a rating and an investigative article all do the same thing to a project: they test its claims against whatever evidence can be found. It is cheaper to run that test yourself, first.

What a review adds

  • Every claim set against the evidence supplied for it: supported, not supported, or not checkable.
  • An attempt to regenerate the headline figures from your own material.
  • The gaps in order of consequence, so that the fixing starts with what matters.

Where it stops

BCCS is not an accredited validation and verification body. A review prepares for verification and cannot stand in for it. It does not apply a registry’s methodology on the registry’s behalf, and it does not involve a field visit.

Evidence readiness review · Try the checklist first

For those who examine

If you verify, rate or insure projects, a re-runnable area record is a second line of evidence that did not originate with the project. It costs you nothing to read, and you can repeat it.

A record states who commissioned it and who paid.

Research groups, NGOs, journalists

You publish a figure, or you are checking one

A reviewer asks how the map was made. A reader asks whether the number can be trusted. A year later a colleague asks which version of the data was used. A record answers all three with files.

What a record adds

  • Inputs frozen with their digests, so “which data” has one answer.
  • A method registered before the result, so “was it tuned” has one answer.
  • A public page others can cite, and a computation they can repeat.

Where it stops

A record is not peer review and does not make a method valid. It makes the work inspectable, which is what a reviewer needs in order to start.

Looking into someone else’s figure?

Start with one question in writing: can this figure be re-run from what was published, and if not, what is missing?

An answer of “it cannot be checked” is a finding, and often the important one.

Have a claim that someone else must be able to check?

Describe the site, the claim and the decision it feeds. You receive a written scope with a deliverable, a date and a price within two working days. If open satellite data cannot answer the question, the reply says so.
