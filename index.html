The problem

An area figure that nobody else can run again is a statement, not evidence

Coastal and blue-carbon finance relies on claims about where mangroves are and whether they persist. A restoration report, a monitoring map or a project description gives the hectares. The data and the steps behind the figure are rarely something a funder, a verifier or a journalist can run for themselves.

Global maps answer a different question

Open global maps are valuable, and they are built for the globe. Global Mangrove Watch v3.0 reports an estimated accuracy of 87.4 % for its extent maps and 58.1 % and 60.6 % for its gain and loss classes.

Bunting et al. (2022), Remote Sensing 14, 3657, as quoted in the programme record of the Observatory.

The party that is checked usually pays the checker

A verifier is engaged by the project it verifies. A record that anyone can re-run does not ask the reader to trust who paid for it: the inputs, the rule and the computation are there to be repeated.

A method that can only say yes proves little

A BCCS record ends in one of three outcomes: supported, with an interval; conflicting; or insufficient evidence. The rule that decides between them is fixed before any data is opened.

How a record is made

The rule comes first. The data comes second.

  1. Fix the question

    The claim, the boundary, the period, the thresholds and the decision rule are written down and hashed. Nothing has been computed yet.

  2. Acquire with receipts

    Open optical and radar scenes and an external reference map are fetched. Every request is recorded with its size and SHA-256.

  3. Compute offline

    The measurement runs from the frozen bytes, with no network. Two sensor groups and the reference are compared under the rule from step one.

  4. Decide, including no

    Each threshold is moved to both ends of its pre-registered bracket. If the answer changes, the outcome is insufficient evidence.

  5. Publish what holds

    Only an approved set leaves the registry. For a refused result, no extent figure is published at all.

What a record is, and what it is not

A record is

  • A dated statement about one area, one period and one question
  • Re-runnable from stored inputs, byte for byte
  • Explicit about uncertainty and about what was not examined
  • Able to end in “insufficient evidence”
  • Marked with who commissioned it and who paid

A record is not

  • A certificate, a carbon credit or a rating
  • A verification under any carbon or biodiversity standard, or the opinion of an accredited verification body
  • Field truth: satellites see canopy, not seedlings, species, biomass or carbon
  • A statement about a site today, unless today’s data was commissioned
  • Independent scientific validation of the method. That has not started, and the public record says so
